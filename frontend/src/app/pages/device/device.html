The unit and how it was measured

A compact ventilation unit with no outdoor part: one reversible radial fan moves the air alternately in and out through a bed of ceramic honeycomb blocks, and an R290 heat pump is built into the same casing. Only the intake/outlet shows on the façade, and no condensate drain is needed – the moisture leaves with the exhaust air.

Air side and measuring points

Laid out after Fig. 1 of the thesis. Point at a sensor to see what it logs; switch the half-cycle.

Refrigerant circuit

Heating mode. R290, 800 g charge; compressor with an inverter, 4-way valve, electronic expansion valve (AKV), one receiver.

Operating variants

  1. Compressor runs in the supply half-cycle with refrigerant through the condenser and the evaporator; off in the exhaust half-cycle.
  2. Compressor runs in the supply half-cycle with refrigerant through the evaporator only; off in the exhaust half-cycle.
  3. As 1, and in the exhaust half-cycle the compressor runs under PID control, 20–90 % (the measured campaign: about 50 %).

One cycle

The fan never stops; a guide vane turns the flow, which takes 4 s, so each reversal has a 2 s dwell. The logger marks the half-cycles with the nawiew and wywiew flags. Heat is counted only in the supply half-cycle, electrical energy over the whole cycle – one 120 s cycle is one COP block.

Ceramic regenerator

Two rows of 15 blocks, 150 × 150 mm each, with 60 × 60 square channels; the rows are 19 mm apart and stand on thin metal tubes. The ceramic stores the heat of the exhaust air for the next supply half-cycle and, being porous, adsorbs the moisture instead of letting it condense.
